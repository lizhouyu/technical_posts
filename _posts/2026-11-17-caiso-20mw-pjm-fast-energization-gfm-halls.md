## At a glance

- **A West-coast ramp number is now on paper.** In its Nov 16 large-load tariff amendment, [CAISO](https://www.caiso.com/ "California Independent System Operator: the grid operator for most of California and a portion of Nevada.") would have each [participating transmission owner](https://www.caiso.com/ "Participating transmission owner (PTO): a utility that turns operation of its transmission over to CAISO.") cap the average active-power ramp of a [Large Load](https://www.caiso.com/library/nov-16-2026-tariff-amendment-large-loads-er27-506 "CAISO proposed definition: an end user at a single site on the CAISO controlled grid with peak load of 50 MW or greater.") at 20 MW/min over a rolling 10-minute interval, in both directions, and restore to 90 percent of pre-disturbance power within 3 seconds after voltage recovers to 0.90 [pu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per-unit_system "Per-unit (pu): a quantity expressed as a fraction of a nominated base, here typically nominal voltage."). [FILI](https://www.caiso.com/documents/nov-16-2026-tariff-amendment-large-loads-er27-532.pdf "Flexible Interim Load Interconnection: CAISO's proposed interim service that can let a large load energize before network upgrades are finished, if it can reduce consumption when transmission capability is unavailable.") can let a hall energize earlier if that flexibility is real. Requested effective date: February 16, 2027. A filed proposal, not an accepted tariff.
- **[PJM](https://insidelines.pjm.com/ "PJM Interconnection: regional grid operator for 13 eastern US states and the District of Columbia.") wants the study clock cut in half.** [Fast Energization](https://insidelines.pjm.com/pjm-proposal-would-cut-interconnection-process-time-in-half/ "PJM's Nov 16 proposal to study new generation and transmission service for large loads on a one-year clock, down from about two years, and to create new transmission services for flexible large loads.") would study generation and large-load transmission together on a one-year path and create services for flexible loads and self-supplied campuses. A separate Nov 16 filing proposes [ride-through](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fault_ride-through "Ride-through: remaining electrically connected, and meeting a prescribed power behavior, during a voltage or frequency disturbance rather than transferring to on-site backup.") so data centers stay on the grid during cleared faults instead of switching to backup. Both are at [FERC](https://www.ferc.gov/ "Federal Energy Regulatory Commission: US agency that regulates interstate transmission and wholesale electricity markets."); not yet accepted.
- **Smoothing shows up on the shelf, still as a vendor claim.** At electronica 2026, [LITEON](https://www.liteon.com/en "LITEON Technology: Taiwanese power-electronics and computing-hardware company.") showed a 110 kW power shelf for [NVIDIA](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/technologies/800-vdc-architecture/ "NVIDIA: designer of GPUs and AI-factory systems, including the Vera Rubin platform.") [Vera Rubin NVL72](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/vera-rubin-nvl72/ "NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72: next-generation rack-scale AI system with 72 Rubin GPUs; successor to Blackwell-class NVL72 racks.") with active current sharing and input-current smoothing, a 72 kW 1U shelf, a [capacitor backup unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercapacitor "Capacitor backup unit (CBU): a short-duration energy store, typically capacitors or supercapacitors, used for hold-up and transient support rather than long backup."), and virtual [800 VDC](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/technologies/800-vdc-architecture/ "800 V direct current: a high-voltage DC distribution architecture for dense AI racks, intended to cut current and conversion stages versus low-voltage AC or 54 V DC.") racks at 330 kW, 880 kW and 1.2 MW (Nov 13).
- **Grid-forming the hall itself, not just the battery, is the new academic control idea.** Zhang, Zhao, Teng and Wang ([arXiv 2611.18428](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18428 "Grid-Forming AI Data Centers: Coordinated Grid Support Control from IT Loads and Energy Storage.")) coordinate IT load and [UPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uninterruptible_power_supply "Uninterruptible power supply: converter-based power conditioner and backup that sits between the grid or generator and the IT load.") storage under [grid-forming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid-forming_inverter "Grid-forming (GFM) control: a converter that behaves as a voltage source and can set frequency and voltage, rather than following the grid with a phase-locked loop.") control. In a 100 MW simulation, maximum [RoCoF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rate_of_change_of_frequency "Rate of change of frequency (RoCoF): how fast system frequency falls or rises after a power imbalance, in hertz per second.") falls from 2.054 to 1.058 Hz/s; a grid-following fast-frequency-response add-on barely moves RoCoF.

## Key developments

### 1. CAISO files a 20 MW/min ramp limit, a 3-second recovery rule, and interim flex while upgrades are built · Nov 16 2026 · industry / grid

CAISO and its participating transmission owners filed a Section 205 large-load tariff amendment on Nov 16, answering FERC's show-cause order. The letter defines a Large Load as an end user at a site on the CAISO controlled grid with peak load of 50 MW or greater. CAISO's library lists the filing as ER27-532. The requested effective date for the core revisions is February 16, 2027. This is a proposal, not an accepted tariff.

The operational clauses are the part that changes hall design. Proposed Section 25.7.1.4 would require each PTO to impose ramp-rate limits during normal operation, start-up, shutdown and restoration: the average active-power ramp, measured at the [point of interconnection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_of_common_coupling "Point of interconnection / point of common coupling: the electrical boundary at which the facility is measured against grid rules.") over a rolling 10-minute interval, must not exceed 20 MW/min, up or down. The limit does not apply to immediate post-disturbance recovery. Separately, proposed Section 25.7.1.3 would require the load to restore active power to at least 90 percent of its pre-disturbance level within 3 seconds after post-fault voltage at the POI recovers to at least 0.90 pu. PTOs would also have to impose voltage and frequency ride-through envelopes, with a CAISO or PTO load-shedding program or remedial action scheme taking precedence — including schemes used to make FILI work.

FILI is the interconnection bargain. If a large load can reduce consumption when transmission capability is unavailable, CAISO says the PTO can permit earlier energization while network upgrades are still pending, subject to eligibility, study, metering, communications, dispatch and compliance requirements so the flexibility is "real and operationally dependable rather than assumed." Permanent flexibility products are flagged as later work. Deposits, milestones and cost-recovery rules are meant to keep speculative projects from shifting upgrade costs onto other customers.

Compared with yesterday's [ISO New England](https://www.iso-ne.com/ "ISO New England: the independent system operator for the six New England states.") three-product tariff (firm service gated on new capacity; interim and non-firm re-dispatch at USD 250/MWh), CAISO writes the same access-for-flexibility deal as an interim interconnection service and then adds a numeric ramp and a sub-10-second recovery obligation. The 20 MW/min cap is tighter than [MISO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midcontinent_Independent_System_Operator "Midcontinent Independent System Operator: grid operator for all or parts of 15 U.S. states and Manitoba.")'s still-pending 30 MW/min computational-load proposal (FERC ER26-3650, covered Nov 10). For a 100 MW IT step held to the allowed ramp, the buffer must still be rated 100 MW; the stored energy is the triangle \\(E_b=\\Delta P^2/(2R)\\), about 4.17 MWh at 20 MW/min versus about 2.78 MWh at 30 MW/min (see Spotlight). A 300 MW campus that must move as a block would need 15 minutes at 20 MW/min — unless start-up is staggered or storage and caps shape the POI trace.

### 2. PJM Fast Energization: study generation and large loads together, and cut the clock from two years to one · Nov 16 2026 · industry / grid

PJM said it would file Fast Energization at FERC at the end of Nov 16. The process is designed to cut the projected time to study new generation and transmission service for large electricity users, including data centers, from two years to one. Generation and load would be studied together. Readiness and financial requirements would be applied earlier, to weed out speculative generation and large-load projects. New transmission services would cover large loads that bring their own generation and flexible loads that can reduce the load they require. States and utilities would get a clearer view of the upgrade costs driven by large loads, so they can decide who pays.

PJM asked for a 60-day comment period and a Commission order within 120 days. As of the Inside Lines note, a link to the filed tariff text had not yet been posted, so treat the one-year clock and the new service menu as PJM's description of the filing, not as an accepted tariff. Fast Energization is a process and product design; it does not, in the public summary, replace a campus ramp or ride-through rule.

The implication for oversubscription is familiar and sharper after ISO-NE and CAISO: a faster study path is being offered to halls that can actually curtail or self-supply. A cap-and-battery stack that can present a smaller, smoother firm slice is what makes that product usable. The 12-month clock is a requested timeline, not a guaranteed interconnect date.

**Sources:** [PJM Inside Lines, Nov 16](https://insidelines.pjm.com/pjm-proposal-would-cut-interconnection-process-time-in-half/ "PJM Plan Would Cut Interconnection Process Time in Half, November 16, 2026.")

### 3. PJM also files ride-through: stay connected through a cleared fault, or the transfer becomes a system event · Nov 16 2026 · industry / grid

In a separate Nov 16 filing, PJM proposed ride-through requirements "to establish thresholds through which data centers must remain connected to the grid, rather than switching to backup generation, to avoid large transfers of load that can destabilize the system." That is the formal FERC submission of a package PJM had previewed on Sept 8-10, after the July 22, 2026 disturbance in which nearly 4,000 MW of data-center load in northern Virginia disconnected and transferred to on-site backup — the third measurable event of that type in two years, in PJM's telling.

The Sept 10 Inside Lines note said the standards would require large computational loads to ride through voltage and frequency excursions from normally cleared transmission faults, with a transition plan for existing facilities. This brief does not repeat those earlier draft envelopes as if they were the filed numbers; the Nov 16 public note confirms that a ride-through filing went in, not the final table of setpoints. CAISO's companion filing, by contrast, does publish a recovery number (90 percent in 3 seconds after 0.90 pu) alongside its ramp cap.

For a hall, ride-through and ramp limits are different bands. Ride-through is about not dropping tens or hundreds of megawatts in a cycle-to-second voltage dip; the 3-second CAISO recovery rule is about coming back without a second shock. Ramp limits are about job starts, stops and scheduled changes over minutes. UPS transfer settings, [static transfer switches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Static_transfer_switch "Static transfer switch: a power-electronic switch that can move a load from one source to another in milliseconds."), GPU caps and facility storage all sit on that stack, and they have to be coordinated so a "successful" transfer to backup is not itself a reliability event.

**Sources:** [PJM Inside Lines, Nov 16 (separate ride-through filing)](https://insidelines.pjm.com/pjm-proposal-would-cut-interconnection-process-time-in-half/ "The Nov 16 Fast Energization note also confirms a separate PJM ride-through filing at FERC.") · [PJM Inside Lines, Sept 10 (proposal preview)](https://insidelines.pjm.com/reliability-standards-to-manage-large-load-disconnection-events-proposed-by-pjm/ "PJM Proposes Reliability Standards to Manage Large Load Disconnection Events, September 10, 2026.")

### 4. LITEON: 110 kW and 72 kW AI shelves, a capacitor backup unit, and virtual 800 VDC racks to 1.2 MW · Nov 13 2026 · industry / rack

At electronica 2026 in Munich (Nov 10-13), LITEON presented a rack-level power portfolio for [NVIDIA MGX](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/products/mgx/ "NVIDIA MGX: a modular server and rack design system for AI and accelerated computing.") and [OCP](https://www.opencompute.org/ "Open Compute Project: industry collaboration that publishes open data-center hardware specifications, including Open Rack and Mt. Diablo.") Open Rack V3. The headline SKUs are a 110 kW power shelf for Vera Rubin NVL72, described as having active current sharing and input-current smoothing "to enhance power stability under dynamic AI workloads," and a 72 kW shelf in a 1U form factor. LITEON also showed a high-power capacitor backup unit and near-chip conversion — [intermediate bus converters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC-to-DC_converter "Intermediate bus converter (IBC): a DC-DC stage that steps a distribution bus down to a lower intermediate voltage for point-of-load regulators."), [voltage regulator modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltage_regulator_module "Voltage regulator module (VRM): the point-of-load converter that feeds a processor or GPU from a lower-voltage bus.") and power bricks — plus an interactive virtual rack for NVIDIA's 800 VDC architecture in 330 kW, 880 kW and 1.2 MW configurations.

This is a trade-show announcement, not a datasheet. There is no published response time, hold-up energy, or measured ramp reduction for the "input-current smoothing" claim, and the 800 VDC racks are a virtual demonstrator. What is useful is the layering: smoothing is being sold at the shelf, a capacitor unit is being offered for short hold-up, and 800 VDC is being sketched at megawatt-rack scale. That is the same split this series has been using — milliseconds and kilowatt-hours are different purchases — now appearing in a volume PSU vendor's portfolio, a step closer to the GPU than facility MV UPS or POI batteries. It does not, by itself, meet a 20 MW/min POI cap.

### 5. Grid-forming AI data centers: coordinate IT caps and UPS storage, and RoCoF falls by about half in simulation · arXiv Nov 13 (announced Nov 16) · academia / grid

Zhang, Zhao, Teng and Wang (Zhejiang University and Imperial College London) argue that grid-following frequency support, which measures frequency through a phase-locked loop and then changes power, is too slow at the inertial stage. They put [virtual synchronous generator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_synchronous_generator "Virtual synchronous generator: a control method that makes a converter imitate the inertia and damping of a synchronous generator.") grid-forming control on both the grid-side converter and the load-side converter, and they share the DC-link voltage error between IT load and UPS energy storage according to a set ratio (50 percent in the case study). An aggregated first-order model of many servers, built from assumed flexibility and time constants by task class, has an equivalent time constant of about 0.2 s inside the band they study.

The test system is a 100 MW AI data center and a 100 MW grid-following wind plant on a 200 MVA, 3 s inertia grid, implemented in MATLAB/Simulink after a PNNL facility report. IT load is 70 MW of a 70 MW set-point, with 20 MW cooling and 10 MW auxiliary; UPS energy-storage rating is 84 MVA, 1.2 times IT power; the DC link is 800 V. Task mix follows an EPRI split: training, fine-tuning and inference at about 30, 10 and 60 percent of IT power, with available reductions of 50, 25 and 10 percent on the deferrable slice of inference (half of inference power). At t = 2 s a sustained 20 MW deficit hits the external grid. Classic grid-following: maximum RoCoF 2.054 Hz/s, nadir 59.46 Hz. Grid-following plus fast frequency response: RoCoF 2.057 Hz/s (unimproved), nadir 59.70 Hz. The proposed scheme: RoCoF 1.058 Hz/s and nadir 59.86 Hz.

The result that matters for capping and smoothing is the RoCoF column. Feedback FFR raises the nadir but does not add inertia; grid-forming the interface does. The IT share eventually meets the 50 percent target, while storage takes more of the first instant because it is faster. This is a single simulated deficit, with assumed task flexibility and no hardware test. It does show a concrete way to spend a 20 MW/min (or tighter) budget: treat IT caps and UPS energy as one GFM plant, not as an after-the-fact limiter on a grid-following UPS.

### 6. Double-conversion UPS can look like a negative resistor across the sub-synchronous band · arXiv Nov 12 (announced Nov 16) · academia / grid

Maharjan, Zheng, Lee, Lwin and Wang (INS Engineering and Iowa State University) ask whether large data-center loads can take part in [sub-synchronous control interaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsynchronous_resonance "Sub-synchronous control interaction (SSCI): an unstable interaction, typically below nominal frequency, between converter controls and the network, originally studied for wind and HVDC plants.") the way wind and HVDC converters can. They scan positive-sequence impedance at the PCC. In a detailed proprietary model, induction-motor, variable-frequency-drive and lighting branches stay passive from 5 Hz to 1 kHz. The UPS branch does not. In bypass, resistance stays slightly positive (about 1-3 mΩ). In [double-conversion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uninterruptible_power_supply#Double-conversion_on-line "Double-conversion UPS: a UPS mode in which all load power passes through a rectifier, a DC link and an inverter, so the IT load is isolated from the AC input.") mode, a nominal 100 MW UPS branch shows about -10 to -15 mΩ from 5 Hz up to the fundamental, remaining negative to roughly 170 Hz. That is the expected constant-power-load behavior inside the control bandwidth. The UPS carries about 80 percent of the facility load in their setup, so the operating mode dominates the risk.

Three public models — PNNL, Texas A&M/ERCOT and the National Laboratory of the Rockies — reproduce the pattern: no negative resistance in bypass, negative resistance only in double-conversion, with a depth and frequency range that depend on the controls. Lighter load makes the negative resistance deeper. Raising DC-link capacitance from 20 to 50 mF on the proprietary model confines the negative band to a narrower region around the fundamental, consistent with a slower DC-voltage loop. The authors recommend screening the UPS branch in double-conversion at partial load during interconnection studies, then a combined load-network scan at the POI if negative resistance appears. There is no time-domain EMT confirmation and no field test in this paper.

The practical overlap with ride-through and 800 VDC work is direct. A hall that lives in double-conversion for power quality is the case that needs an SSCI screen; a bypassed UPS is not. Slowing the DC-voltage loop or adding capacitance is a mitigation knob, and it trades against the same control bandwidth that people want for fast load following. Interconnection packages that already demand EMT models now have a specific scan to run, not only a ride-through case list.

**Sources:** [arXiv:2611.17550](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17550 "Sub-Synchronous Control Interaction Risk of Large Data Center Loads: A Frequency-Scan Assessment with Cross-Model Validation.")

### 7. Dispatch the UPS for inertia and primary frequency reserve, not only for backup · arXiv Nov 13 (announced Nov 16) · academia / grid

Wang (University of Arkansas at Little Rock) and Du (Villanova University) put data-center UPS flexibility into a frequency-security-aware real-time economic dispatch. The model jointly schedules energy, [primary frequency response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancillary_services_(electric_power) "Primary frequency response (PFR): the automatic, seconds-scale change in real power that arrests a frequency deviation after a disturbance.") reserves and inertia, with analytical RoCoF and nadir limits written as a second-order cone program. On a modified IEEE 39-bus system with six synchronous machines and four UPS aggregators, a one-hour, 12-interval schedule against a scaled CAISO net-load profile costs USD 79,245 in their units. UPS units charge in light intervals and discharge in heavy ones, and they are used for extra virtual inertia only in the two most severe intervals.

Table I compares frequency metrics with the same machine reserve schedule, with and without UPS inertia and PFR. Maximum RoCoF falls from 0.5900 to 0.5000 Hz/s (the paper's limit; 15.26 percent). Maximum frequency deviation falls from 5.4216 Hz to 0.4000 Hz (92.62 percent), which is what keeps the case inside the paper's 0.4 Hz nadir limit and, in their framing, avoids under-frequency load shedding. The 5.42 Hz no-UPS deviation is an extremely large excursion: it is the paper's modeled deficit on this test system, not a measured event, and it is the reason the UPS term looks so decisive. Simulation only; the UPS is treated as a voluntary, costed resource, and the authors note that incentives for that participation are still to be designed.

Read next to the GFM paper: one work shapes the converter interface so the hall supports frequency by construction; the other asks the market to pay UPS headroom for inertia and PFR on a 5-minute dispatch. Both assume the IT load can tolerate the power being taken from, or stored in, the UPS.

**Sources:** [arXiv:2611.17691](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17691 "Frequency-Security-Aware Real-Time Economic Dispatch with Data Center UPS Flexibility for Inertia and Primary Frequency Response Services.")

### 8. Carbon-aware flexibility of distributed data centers: cleaner sites get the wider band · arXiv Nov 13 (announced Nov 16) · adjacent

Wang and Chen (Chinese University of Hong Kong) quantify the power flexibility a fleet of distributed data centers can offer as a Cartesian product of adjustable demand intervals, under a system-level carbon constraint, and do it online with [Lyapunov optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyapunov_optimization "Lyapunov optimization: a method for making real-time control decisions that keep queues stable while approaching an objective, without a full forecast.") so that uncertain arrivals and a moving grid carbon intensity do not have to be forecast perfectly. Dispatch from the system operator is disaggregated back into site decisions and then used to update future flexibility.

The case is a one-month, 720-hour simulation with eight front-end nodes and four back-end data centers, Google cluster traces, and Electricity Maps carbon intensities, with a 10 tCO2/h emission-rate target and 30 MWh site energy bounds. When flexibility is valued uniformly, the method delivers 44,701 MWh of cumulative flexibility at exactly 10.00 tCO2/h, versus 21,652 MWh (9.14 tCO2/h) for a static myopic baseline and 11,557 MWh (8.39 tCO2/h) for an adaptive myopic baseline. Cleaner sites receive more of the band: the two lowest-intensity sites (0.2016 and 0.2282 kgCO2/kWh) contribute 13,672 and 13,645 MWh, against 4,690 MWh at the dirtiest site (0.4911 kgCO2/kWh). The traces are general cluster workloads, not a measured LLM power model, and the result is simulated.

The link to today's tariffs is that "flexible interim" service only works if someone can name a real, time-varying band and then follow a dispatch. This paper is one way to compute that band when a carbon cap is also binding. It does not replace a POI ramp or ride-through rule, and it will overstate available flex if the workload is interactive inference with little deferral.

**Sources:** [arXiv:2611.17714](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17714 "Online Carbon-Aware Power Flexibility Quantification of Distributed Data Centers.")

## Spotlight

Hold a POI ramp to a limit \\(R\\) while IT load steps by \\(\\Delta P\\): the buffer must supply \\(P_b(t)=\\Delta P-R t\\) until \\(t=\\Delta P/R\\), so the energy is \\(E_b=\\Delta P^2/(2R)\\). At CAISO's proposed 20 MW/min, a 100 MW step takes 5 minutes and about 4.17 MWh; at MISO's pending 30 MW/min it takes 3.3 minutes and about 2.78 MWh. The buffer's power rating is still 100 MW in both cases. Halving the step quarters the energy. CAISO's 90 percent / 3-second post-fault recovery is a different band and is not stored by this triangle.

## Bottom line

The Nov 16 show-cause deadline produced a number, not just a process. CAISO's 20 MW/min, 10-minute-average ramp and 3-second recovery rule are now design inputs for West-coast halls, if FERC accepts them. PJM is offering a one-year study path and a ride-through filing, in the same access-for-flexibility bargain ISO-NE wrote yesterday as a three-product tariff. None of these filings is accepted yet. What makes them usable is a hall that can actually curtail, restore and smooth at the POI — with IT caps and UPS or shelf storage acting together, as the grid-forming paper sketches, and with double-conversion UPS impedance in the interconnection model, as the SSCI paper recommends. Shelf-level "input-current smoothing" is appearing in vendor portfolios; it has not yet been shown to meet a 20 MW/min grid cap.
