## At a glance

- **ISO-NE is first to file a large-load tariff at [FERC](https://www.ferc.gov/ "Federal Energy Regulatory Commission: US agency that regulates interstate transmission and wholesale electricity markets.").** [ISO New England](https://www.iso-ne.com/ "ISO New England: the independent system operator for the six New England states.") would offer firm service only with new capacity and network upgrades; interim and permanent non-firm loads would be re-dispatched at USD 250/MWh (Nov 13; process starts June 1, 2027). Not yet accepted.
- **The last meter before the rack gets a standard SKU.** [Vertiv](https://www.vertiv.com/ "Vertiv: US maker of data-center power and thermal equipment (UPS, PDUs, busway, cooling).") launched the PowerStation 3100 [RPP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_panel "Remote power panel (RPP): the last distribution panel before the rack, typically in the white space."), a 600-1,200 A [UL 891](https://www.ul.com/ "UL 891: standard for switchboards used in electrical distribution.") platform for racks above 100 kW, with per-rack leak isolation. Available now in North America (Nov 13).
- **Speed-to-power is being prepaid.** [Fermi](https://investor.fermiamerica.com/ "Fermi Inc. (NASDAQ: FRMI): developer of the Project Matador energy-and-data-center campus near Amarillo, Texas.") announced agreements with an unnamed investment-grade hyperscaler for an initial 600 MW at Project Matador, with a USD 200 million prepayment — and no signed lease yet (Nov 12).
- **A battery can buy a faster interconnect.** At the [BNEF](https://about.bnef.com/ "BloombergNEF: Bloomberg's energy research unit.") Houston summit, Google's Tyler Norris cited a 31 MW / 62 MWh battery that helped a 72 MW hall skip a fully firmed network upgrade (reported Nov 13).
- **Academia, at the GPU and at the point of connection.** PACER (arXiv 2611.17206) manages [EDP](https://www.opencompute.org/ "Electrical Design Power peaks (EDP p): short overshoots above a GPU's thermal design power (TDP).") peaks and improves median [TTFT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_to_first_token "Time to first token: latency from request arrival to the first generated token.") by up to 12% on dense models. A 1 GW [STATCOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Static_synchronous_compensator "Static synchronous compensator: a converter that injects or absorbs reactive power to support voltage.")-plus-[UPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uninterruptible_power_supply "Uninterruptible power supply: equipment that keeps critical loads powered through grid disturbances.") study (arXiv 2611.16569) shows the limits of voltage support in simulation.
- **Adjacent:** a CMU/Berkeley technoeconomic model finds that DC-coupled solar-plus-storage microgrids win in 85% of modeled U.S. locations once faster construction is valued (arXiv 2611.17098). Oregon State and the National Laboratory of the Rockies model 371 Texas projects (38.9 GW by 2030) and attribute 68% of a 1.64 cents/kWh residential increase to wholesale prices (arXiv 2611.16095).

## Key developments

### 1. ISO-NE files the first large-load tariff: firm service needs new capacity; flexible loads re-dispatch at USD 250/MWh · Nov 13 2026 · industry / grid

[ISO New England](https://www.iso-ne.com/ "ISO New England: the independent system operator for the six New England states."), the [New England Power Pool](https://nepool.com/ "New England Power Pool (NEPOOL): the stakeholder body for the New England wholesale electricity market."), and the Participating Transmission Owners Administrative Committee jointly filed a Section 205 proposal with [FERC](https://www.ferc.gov/ "Federal Energy Regulatory Commission: US agency that regulates interstate transmission and wholesale electricity markets.") to create a defined application and study process for large loads such as data centers. The process would be implemented on June 1, 2027. The operator's Nov 9 information summary, which describes the proposal now filed, defines a Large Load as a commercial or industrial customer at a single site with gross peak demand of 50 MW or greater, interconnecting above 60 kV, with exemptions for some existing and critical loads and a transition if an existing load grows by 50 MW or more relative to its exempt demand as of June 18, 2026.

The filing creates three transmission-service options. **Firm contract demand service** has the same priority as other firm load, but requires network upgrades and a "bring your own new capacity" match. **Interim contract demand service** is a non-firm path while the customer works toward firm service. **Permanent non-firm contract demand service** is for flexible loads that do not designate new capacity. ISO-NE would "re-dispatch" non-firm and interim customers whenever energy prices in their area reach USD 250/MWh, by cutting consumption or increasing eligible generation. The ISO says the region currently crosses that threshold in only about 1% of hours, and that the trigger is meant to instruct a reduction before reliability risk from thinner upgrades and no capacity supply. Non-firm and interim service would be excluded from the Installed Capacity Requirement calculation. Stakeholder work on the detailed bring-your-own-capacity rules continues in 2027. ISO Newswire frames the package as designed to preserve reliability and avoid shifting costs to other consumers.

This is a filed proposal, not an accepted tariff. Secondary reports of the transmittal add a docket number, fees, and a later service-start date; those figures are not on ISO Newswire, so they are omitted here. The binding idea is already on the first-party page: firm service is gated on new supply and upgrades; faster access is explicitly interruptible at a published price. That is the interconnection-for-flexibility bargain this series has been tracking, now written as a three-product tariff — and the first of the six FERC large-load show-cause responses to land, ahead of the Nov 16 deadline still facing [CAISO](https://www.caiso.com/ "California Independent System Operator: grid operator for most of California."), [PJM](https://www.pjm.com/ "PJM Interconnection: regional grid operator for 13 eastern US states and DC."), [MISO](https://www.misoenergy.org/ "Midcontinent Independent System Operator: grid operator for all or parts of 15 U.S. states and Manitoba."), and [NYISO](https://www.nyiso.com/ "New York Independent System Operator: grid operator for New York state.").

Sources: [ISO Newswire, Nov 13](https://isonewswire.com/2026/11/13/iso-ne-files-proposal-with-ferc-to-address-large-loads/ "ISO-NE files proposal with FERC to address large loads.") · [ISO-NE Large Loads Integration Proposal Information Summary, Nov 9](https://www.iso-ne.com/static-assets/documents/100040/large_loads_integration_proposal_information_summary_10.09.26.pdf "Official summary of the Large Loads Integration proposal, last updated November 9, 2026.")

### 2. Vertiv PowerStation 3100 RPP: 600-1,200 A on one platform for 100 kW+ racks · Nov 13 2026 · industry / rack

On Nov 13, Vertiv introduced the PowerStation 3100 remote power panel, the last distribution stage before the rack in its AI power path. A single hardware platform covers 600 A, 800 A, 1,000 A and 1,200 A, which Vertiv says is three to five times the capacity of traditional panelboard-based RPPs. It is delivered as a complete [UL 891](https://www.ul.com/ "UL 891: standard for switchboards used in electrical distribution.") listed assembly, is available now in North America, and is designed to support both fully AC distribution and DC rack architectures so operators can standardize across deployment types.

The design targets two problems that appear as racks move past 100 kW. Four-wire delivery at 415 V or 480 V can raise available fault current, so the panel uses molded-case breakers with higher short-circuit withstand and interrupting ratings (the product documentation cited in trade coverage specifies a 100%-rated main breaker with a 65 kA interrupting capacity and no full-load derating, plus space for as many as 22 three-pole branch breakers — treat those figures as vendor documentation, not an independent test). Direct liquid cooling adds a different requirement: each branch breaker includes an integrated remote [shunt trip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shunt_trip "Shunt trip: an accessory that opens a breaker when an external signal, such as a leak detector, is asserted."), so a leak-detection system can isolate one rack without taking down neighbors or the pod. Adjustable trip units let operators switch among 60 A, 100 A and 150 A within the same breaker frame.

Vendor launch, not a field result. The 3-5× capacity claim is versus traditional panelboards, and the DC-rack support is an architecture claim, not a statement that this panel is itself an 800 VDC bus. For oversubscription, the useful pieces are selectable trip ratings as rack power changes and per-rack isolation that does not collapse a pod's power budget. Metering at 0.5% accuracy, if it holds in the brochure, is also the telemetry a cap or ramp controller needs at this boundary.

Sources: [Vertiv press release, Nov 13](https://www.vertiv.com/en-us/about/news-and-events/corporate-news/2026/vertiv-introduces-high-density-remote-power-panel-for-next-generation-ai-data-centers/ "Vertiv introduces the PowerStation 3100 RPP for next-generation AI data centers.") · [PowerStation 3100 RPP product page](https://www.vertiv.com/en-us/products-catalog/critical-power/power-distribution/vertiv-powerstation-3100-rpp-high-density-remote-power-panel-600a-to-1200a/ "Vertiv PowerStation 3100 RPP product page, 600 A to 1,200 A.")

### 3. Fermi: 600 MW of prepaid power for an unnamed hyperscaler, still no signed lease · Nov 12 2026 · industry / grid

Fermi announced, with its third-quarter results, agreements with an undisclosed investment-grade hyperscale customer to advance an initial 600 MW of power capacity at Project Matador, beginning in 2027. The customer is making financial and development commitments *ahead of finalizing a lease*: a USD 200 million prepayment and a USD 15 million early-construction and site-development agreement. A USD 90 million structural-steel and materials purchase agreement is still being finalized. CEO Lee McIntire tied the deal to "speed to power" and said that if Fermi converts its pipeline of prospective customers into leases, it would fill 2.2 GW of current capacity. The customer's identity is confidential. Treat later press reports that name a specific hyperscaler as unconfirmed.

The same release states that Fermi and [TensorWave](https://tensorwave.com/ "TensorWave: an AI cloud provider.") have terminated the data-center lease and services agreement signed in August 2026, and that the associated [AMD](https://www.amd.com/ "Advanced Micro Devices: designer of CPUs and Instinct data-center GPUs.") credit-support agreement is also terminated. The campus remains pre-revenue and pre-lease. Company commentary about a much larger theoretical campus should be treated as a development ambition, not energized capacity.

The power is being reserved before the building lease exists. That is the industry counterpart of today's technoeconomic paper (item 7): customers will prepay to secure megawatts on a date, and a campus that cannot present a firm, ramp-compliant load at the [POI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_of_common_coupling "Point of interconnection / point of common coupling: the electrical boundary where the campus meets the utility or ISO-operated grid.") will have a harder time converting a prepayment into a lease.

Sources: [Fermi announcement via Newswire, Nov 12](https://www.newswire.com/news/fermi-announces-new-agreements-with-investment-grade-hyperscaler-and-22888329 "Fermi Announces New Agreements with Investment-Grade Hyperscaler and Third Quarter 2026 Financial Results.") · [Fermi investor site](https://investor.fermiamerica.com/ "Fermi America investor relations.")

### 4. Non-firm transmission and a 31 MW / 62 MWh battery as an interconnection ticket · Nov 13 2026 · industry / grid

[Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/ "Utility Dive: trade publication covering US electric utilities and grid policy."), reporting on a BNEF Houston summit panel held Wednesday, quotes Tyler Norris, Google's head of market innovation for energy and power: estimated grid utilization of 40% to 60%, with the "real whitespace" in enabling non-firm transmission service. Large-load customers will offer flexible capacity if it gets them connected faster, he said, citing an agreement between [Aligned Data Centers](https://aligneddc.com/ "Aligned Data Centers: a US wholesale data-center operator.") and [Portland General Electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_General_Electric "Portland General Electric (PGE): the investor-owned utility serving much of Oregon, including Hillsboro.") in which the developer agreed to deploy a 31 MW / 62 MWh battery for a 72 MW data center in Hillsboro, Oregon, "to bypass what would have otherwise required a fully firmed network upgrade." Norris added, "We will see more" of these arrangements.

Other figures on the same panel are context, not new measurements: [Dominion Energy](https://www.dominionenergy.com/ "Dominion Energy: US utility, including the Virginia service territory with a large data-center load.")'s Virginia subsidiary recently reported a distribution-grid average load factor of 41% and a transmission "median facility utilization" of about 33%; [NRG](https://www.nrg.com/ "NRG Energy: a Texas-based independent power producer and retailer.")'s Jessica Hogle stated a 25 GW demand-response, storage and virtual-power-plant goal by 2035. Arushi Sharma Frank, an advisor to [Emerald AI](https://www.emeraldai.co/ "Emerald AI: a company that builds software for grid-interactive, flexible data-center loads."), argued for software-based flexibility inside utility planning.

Speaker comments at a conference, plus one concrete battery-for-interconnect example. The 40-60% utilization band is Norris's estimate; [Grid Strategies](https://gridstrategiesllc.com/ "Grid Strategies: a consultancy that publishes an annual report on US bulk-power supply and demand.")'s systemwide load-factor figure quoted in the same article is about 60%, heading to 66% by 2030. The Aligned battery is 31 MW on a 72 MW hall — not a full ride-through of the campus — and 62 MWh is two hours at that 31 MW rating. For capping and ramp smoothing, this is a contract path: a named MW/MWh flexibility resource in exchange for a faster, less fully firmed interconnection, which is only usable if the hall can actually follow the battery and any curtailment terms.

Sources: [Utility Dive, Nov 13](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/utility-tech-officials-see-opportunities-to-increase-grid-utilization/874644/ "Utility, tech officials see opportunities to increase grid utilization, reporting on a BNEF Houston Summit panel.")

### 5. PACER: managing GPU power excursions improves inference TTFT by up to 12% · arXiv Nov 12 (announced Nov 13) · academia / server

Jain, Morris, Jiang, Das, Kao, Paul, Venkataraman, Manne and Sinclair (University of Wisconsin-Madison and [AMD](https://www.amd.com/ "Advanced Micro Devices: designer of CPUs and Instinct data-center GPUs.")) characterize and then manage [EDP p](https://www.opencompute.org/ "Electrical Design Power peaks: short overshoots above TDP.") — short power overshoots above a GPU's TDP — on [Instinct MI300X](https://www.amd.com/en/products/accelerators/instinct/mi300.html "AMD Instinct MI300X: a 750 W TDP data-center GPU used in the PACER evaluation.") (750 W TDP). They argue that modern [LLM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_language_model "Large language model.") workloads, with short kernels and rapidly changing compute demand, interact poorly with coarse dynamic power management: sustained peaks trigger throttling and intra-GPU performance variability. Table 1(a) quotes the OCP duration ladder in Figure 1.

In hardware, clamping the acceptable EDP p threshold from 1.0× to 1.5× TDP shows that some overshoot (they highlight about 1.3× TDP) is useful computational sprinting; clamping all the way to TDP costs performance. In firmware, a graphics-frequency cap reduces high-magnitude EDP p events and improves performance by 3-7% over baseline GPU power management on offline inference, coming within 3% of an oracle per-kernel cap. On 8-GPU online serving, a lookup table of frequency caps by batched-token bucket, applied each scheduler epoch, reduces median TTFT by 9-12% across request rates for LLaMA 3.1 405B, by 4-5% for Mistral-Medium-3.5-128B (SLO attainment up to about 2%), and by at most 1.2% for DeepSeek V3.2, whose predicted caps already sit near the uncapped 2,100 MHz ceiling. Lessons learned: for dense, GEMM-dominated models, PACER improves TTFT by up to 12% and SLO attainment by up to 6%; mixture-of-experts models with fractured kernel mixes gain much less. The work also notes prior observations that EDP p complicates power-oversubscription schemes that assume a fixed, monitorable budget at rack level or above.

Implication: a hall- or rack-level cap at TDP is not the same constraint the GPU actually violates. The exploitable headroom for inference oversubscription is reduced if frequent EDP p events cause throttling and TTFT jitter. Firmware frequency caps are a complementary knob to the decode/prefill caps in earlier briefs: they stabilize the microsecond-to-millisecond band those software caps never see. Single-vendor GPU family; several gains are model-dependent.

Sources: [arXiv 2611.17206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17206 "PACER: GPU Excursion Management for ML Workloads.")

### 6. STATCOM and UPS voltage support help milder faults, not deep sags · arXiv Nov 12 (announced Nov 13) · academia / grid

Liu, Liu and Hou (University of Hong Kong) ask whether a STATCOM and a coordinated AIDC UPS can limit the drop in grid power intake during fault-induced voltage sags. They run electromagnetic-transient simulations of a 1 GW [PNNL](https://www.pnnl.gov/ "Pacific Northwest National Laboratory.") DC2 model — 1,050 UPS units, each on a 1 MVA, 0.48 kV AC / 1.2 kV DC base — connected at Bus 8 of an IEEE nine-bus system in [PSCAD/EMTDC](https://www.pscad.com/ "PSCAD/EMTDC: an electromagnetic-transient simulation tool used for power-system studies."). Native [momentary cessation](https://www.nerc.com/ "Momentary cessation (MC): a converter protective response that temporarily stops current exchange during a severe voltage disturbance without necessarily opening the point of connection.") blocks the rectifier when the minimum local phase-RMS voltage stays below 0.7 pu for 10 ms, and resets after the voltage exceeds 0.9 pu for 50 ms. The native current walk-in is 4 s. Coordinated UPS control holds a fixed active-power reference during support and gives leftover current rating to reactive support.

On milder tested faults, STATCOM support better preserves short-term intake; UPS coordination keeps the rectifier in operation under deeper sags by cutting active intake to free reactive current. Combined support improves auxiliary-load retention and recovery in selected cases. For a 200 ms fault at 3.03 ohm, both coordinated configurations avoid cessation and keep auxiliary loads, but recovery differs: combined support cuts full-event battery discharge from 293.549 MJ to 193.705 MJ (a 34.01% reduction) and advances sustained 90% recovery from 0.7873 s to 0.5528 s after fault application (Figure 2). A 200 ms fault with STATCOM support alone still produces 75.7 ms of cessation. Under deeper sags, all four configurations enter cessation, and STATCOM-equipped cases can spend *longer* in it. Two additional 100 ms near faults with uncompensated PCC voltages of about 0.5 pu and 0.3 pu see only slight voltage improvement with combined support (to 0.50839 pu and 0.30643 pu); cessation still occurs. Simulation only; a single test system; control settings were not retuned for the deepest sags.

Implication: ride-through and ramp-limited reconnection, already a theme in this series, cannot be solved by "add a STATCOM" alone. Coordinated UPS reactive support can shrink the battery energy spent on a moderate event, which is a smoothing and recovery benefit. Severe sags still shed grid demand. GPU caps and staged reconnection remain relevant so the return-to-grid step itself does not become the next transient.

### 7. Speed-to-power, not unit energy cost, decides how AI campuses get powered · arXiv Nov 12 (announced Nov 13) · adjacent

Newkirk, Gerber, Fuchs, Hubbard, Morgan, Shehabi, Smith and Samaras (Carnegie Mellon University and UC Berkeley) compare four ways of powering an AI data center across the continental United States: AC-coupled solar-plus-storage, DC-coupled solar-plus-storage, islanded natural gas with diesel backup, and grid interconnection. The open-source model uses empirically calibrated AI *training* loads (a fleet of 8-[H100](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/h100/ "NVIDIA H100: a Hopper-generation data-center GPU. The paper models 8-H100 node equivalents at 7.3 kW average and 8.5 kW maximum.") node equivalents at 7.3 kW average and 8.5 kW maximum), climate-driven cooling, stage-by-stage conversion losses and physics-based battery degradation. Their central construct is a speed-adjusted cost of energy that adds the value of compute unlocked by earlier operation, so options with different construction timelines can be compared on one basis. Solar-plus-storage is modeled on a 24-month timeline.

DC coupling reduces lifetime energy costs by 18% (9 cents/kWh) on average versus AC counterparts, by eliminating inverters and improving efficiency. DC-coupled microgrids are rarely cheapest on *unadjusted* energy cost, but they become the lowest-cost modeled option in 85% of U.S. locations once foregone compute is valued, or in 55% of locations under accelerated natural gas timelines. Sensitivity work includes 4,317 locations; a 1 cent/kWh near-indifference band covers only 3.5% of them in the base case. The paper is a training-calibrated, hourly energy-balance study, not a model of millisecond ramps or inference SLO caps, and construction timelines and rental-rate assumptions drive the speed adjustment.

Implication: the Fermi prepayment and the Aligned/PGE battery are the industry rhyme. A hall that can connect sooner — even with a less "optimal" energy mix, or by offering storage and curtailment — can beat a cheaper but slower firm interconnection. DC coupling's 18% energy-cost edge also argues for keeping conversion stages off the critical path as 800 VDC facility designs mature. Do not import the 85% figure as a siting rule for inference halls without re-running the speed adjustment on a different utilization and timeline.

Sources: [arXiv 2611.17098](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17098 "Speed-to-Power Dominates Energy Costs for AI Data Centers: Technoeconomic Analysis of Powering AI in the Continental United States.")


### 8. In Texas, most of the modeled household bill hit comes from wholesale prices, not transmission charges · arXiv Nov 11 (announced Nov 13) · adjacent

Zhou, Xia, Wang, Ding and Zhu (Oregon State University and the [National Laboratory of the Rockies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Renewable_Energy_Laboratory "National Laboratory of the Rockies: the DOE laboratory formerly known as NREL, Golden, Colorado.")) quantify how 371 announced Texas data-center projects, totaling 38.9 GW by 2030, could reach residential bills. They link power-system expansion, wholesale markets, transmission reinforcement and retail cost allocation. In one representative modeled case, accommodating this demand increases residential electricity costs by 1.64 cents/kWh, of which 68% comes from higher wholesale prices and 32% from regulated transmission charges. Allowing new gas generation near data centers reduces the 2023 residential cost increase to 0.92 cents/kWh; excluding data centers from the transmission cost-sharing base raises it to 1.87 cents/kWh. The authors' conclusion is that the residential burden is driven primarily by wholesale price increases rather than by direct transmission investment, and that the outcome depends on generation siting, planning and cost allocation.

This is a modeled Texas case, not a measurement, and it complements the Nov 11 *Nature Energy* household-burden result with an explicit split between wholesale and transmission channels. It does not model ramp limits or curtailment tariffs. Read alongside ISO-NE's "avoid shifting costs to other consumers" framing: a campus that brings its own capacity, or that can be re-dispatched in high-priced hours, is arguing about both of those channels.

Sources: [arXiv 2611.16095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16095 "Data-center growth impacts household electricity costs more through wholesale prices than through grid reinforcement.")

## Spotlight

A rack-level cap at TDP and a POI ramp limit live in different time bands. Figure 1's OCP ladder allows 2× TDP for only 20 microseconds and 1.1× TDP for 20 milliseconds; PACER shows that *some* of that overshoot is useful sprinting, and that the penalty for unmanaged peaks is throttling and TTFT jitter, not a clean clip at TDP. Hours-to-months are a different resource: a 31 MW / 62 MWh battery is two hours at 31 MW, and a prepaid 600 MW block is a 2027 delivery date. Buy the microsecond band (firmware, PoL, rack breakers), the second-to-minute band (UPS, STATCOM, sidecars), and the interconnection date separately. ISO-NE now puts a price on the last of those: USD 250/MWh re-dispatch for loads that did not bring new capacity.

## Bottom line

ISO-NE is first to write the flexibility bargain into a three-product tariff: firm service needs new capacity; everyone else is re-dispatched at USD 250/MWh. The rack boundary now has a shipping high-density RPP with leak-triggered isolation; the GPU still violates TDP on a timescale no rack cap can see, and managing those excursions is worth up to 12% median TTFT on dense models. Grid access continues to trade on flexibility and speed — a prepaid 600 MW block without a lease, a 31 MW battery that deferred a firmed upgrade — which is exactly what the speed-to-power result says one should expect. A Texas bill-impact model says most of the household hit comes through wholesale prices, not transmission charges. Ride-through support helps moderate faults and does not cancel deep ones.
