## At a glance

- **Faster [VLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vision-language-action_model "Vision-language-action (VLA) model: a policy that maps camera input and language to robot actions.") calls can run hotter.** [Ixian-bench](https://ixian.zetic.ai/ "Ixian-bench: a public dashboard of Physical AI measurements on Jetson boards, published by ZETIC.") finds that on a [Jetson AGX Thor](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/autonomous-machines/embedded-systems/jetson-thor/ "NVIDIA Jetson AGX Thor: a high-end embedded computer for robots and edge AI."), [torch.compile](https://pytorch.org/tutorials/intermediate/torch_compile_tutorial.html "torch.compile: PyTorch’s just-in-time compiler, used here with the Inductor backend.") cuts π0.5 energy per call from 16.74 J to 11.31 J while raising average board power from 68.2 W to 86.5 W.
- **State-aware detection helps power, not always energy.** Basit and Shafique (arXiv [2611.07663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07663 "Basit and Shafique, Energy-Aware Open-Vocabulary Detection Scheduling, arXiv 2611.07663, submitted 5 November 2026. A preprint, not peer reviewed.")) cut mean Jetson input power 5.2% versus an 8 Hz baseline on a real [AgileX LIMO](https://github.com/agilexrobotics/limo_ros2 "AgileX LIMO: a compact wheeled research robot used as the paper’s mobile platform; official ROS 2 repository."), and warn that longer missions can erase energy savings.
- **Two cooling twins, neither a live control loop.** [Vertiv](https://www.vertiv.com/ "Vertiv: a vendor of data-center power, cooling and controls.")’s [ATLAS](https://www.vertiv.com/en-us/about/news-and-events/corporate-news/2026/vertiv-atlas-proves-ai-infrastructure-ahead-of-silicon/ "Vertiv ATLAS (Advanced Test Lab for Architecture and Systems): a pod-scale lab in Columbus, Ohio, announced 16 November 2026.") lab opened on 6 November with a recorded digital twin and load-bank tests; full dynamic emulation is due in early 2027. [ExaDigiT](https://exadigit.github.io/ "ExaDigiT: an open-source framework for digital twins of liquid-cooled supercomputers, led from Oak Ridge National Laboratory.") presented cooling surrogates at [SC26](https://sc26.supercomputing.org/ "SC26: the International Conference for High Performance Computing, Networking, Storage and Analysis, held 15–20 November 2026 in Chicago."); the checkable public paper is still pre-surrogate analysis.

## Key developments

### 1. On a Jetson, compile can raise watts and cut joules per VLA call · 28 Oct 2026 · industry / robot compute

ZETIC, a vendor of edge-inference optimization, posted board-level measurements of π0.5, SmolVLA and GR00T N1.7 on Jetson boards including AGX Thor (28 October). Power is whole-board [tegrastats](https://docs.nvidia.com/jetson/archives/r39.2.1/DeveloperGuide/AT/JetsonLinuxDevelopmentTools/TegrastatsUtility.html "tegrastats: NVIDIA’s on-board utility for reporting Jetson power, temperature and utilization.") over a roughly 60-second window at pinned [MAXN](https://docs.nvidia.com/jetson/archives/r38.4/DeveloperGuide/SD/PlatformPowerAndPerformance/JetsonThor.html "MAXN: an unconstrained Jetson power mode that enables maximum cores and clocks; sustained performance can still be thermally limited.") clocks; energy per call is average power times duration, divided by inference count. On Thor, BF16 batch-1, torch.compile takes π0.5 from 254 ms and 16.74 J at 68.2 W to 132 ms and 11.31 J at 86.5 W, and SmolVLA from 8.58 J at 43.4 W to 3.35 J at 66.9 W, while raising SmolVLA peak memory from 4.22 GB to 6.76 GB. [TensorRT](https://developer.nvidia.com/tensorrt "NVIDIA TensorRT: an inference optimizer and runtime; not included in these published rows.") was not measured, and the rows are not physical-robot validations.

Implication: For on-board VLA inference, the battery budget and the thermal budget can move in opposite directions: a faster path that draws more watts may still use fewer joules per call, and may change whether the model fits the board.

**Sources:** [NVIDIA Developer Forums, 28 Oct](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/vla-models-0-5-gr00t-smolvla-measured-on-jetson-agx-thor-agx-orin-and-orin-nano-latency-energy-memory-per-policy-call/387191 "Primary source for the Thor numbers, clock pinning, tegrastats window, and the watts-versus-joules observation."); [ixian-bench methodology](https://ixian.zetic.ai/methodology "Measurement rules for latency, energy and evidence states.").

### 2. An energy-aware detector schedule cuts power on a real robot, not always mission energy · submitted 5 Nov 2026 · academia / robot compute

Abdul Basit and Muhammad Shafique at [NYU Abu Dhabi](https://nyuad.nyu.edu/ "New York University Abu Dhabi.") (arXiv [2611.07663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07663 "Basit and Shafique, arXiv 2611.07663, a preprint, not peer reviewed.")) switch [YOLO-World-S](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17270 "YOLO-World: an open-vocabulary detector that can ground text queries for classes not fixed at training time; the paper uses the Small variant.") among low-, mid- and high-power modes on a Jetson Orin Nano from odometry, map change and detector confidence. In 40 LIMO navigation trials it records 8/10 mission success against 7/10 for each fixed-rate baseline, 5.2% lower mean Jetson input power versus an 8 Hz baseline, and a 34.0% lower call rate. They state that this does not minimize mission-window energy, because longer missions can erase instantaneous savings. A 60-second stationary microbenchmark shows mean power falling from 8.045 W to 7.301 W and energy from 484.7 J to 438.1 J.

Implication: Energy-aware perception should be scored at task completion, not only at instantaneous watts: a lower call rate that stretches the mission can spend as many, or more, joules to finish.

**Sources:** [arXiv 2611.07663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07663 "Abstract: 5.2%, 34.0%, 8/10 versus 7/10, and the mission-energy caveat.") and the [HTML full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07663 "Source of the 40-trial design and the 8.045 W / 7.301 W and 484.7 J / 438.1 J microbenchmark.").

### 3. Vertiv’s ATLAS lab tests power and cooling as a system, still on 2026 bulk loads · 6–18 Nov 2026 · industry / digital twin

Vertiv’s 16 November [release](https://www.vertiv.com/en-us/about/news-and-events/corporate-news/2026/vertiv-atlas-proves-ai-infrastructure-ahead-of-silicon/ "Vertiv, 16 November 2026, Columbus, Ohio.") describes ATLAS as a representative pod that uses emulation, digital modeling in [NVIDIA DSX](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/products/dsx/sim/ "NVIDIA DSX Sim: NVIDIA’s AI-factory design and simulation platform, including Omniverse libraries and SimReady assets.") and physical tests to reproduce the power and heat of [NVIDIA](https://www.nvidia.com/ "NVIDIA: the GPU vendor whose DSX platform and compute architectures are cited in the ATLAS materials.") architectures before those systems ship. There are no NVIDIA GPUs in the lab; emulators stand in for unreleased silicon. A 6 November ribbon-cutting, recounted on [18 November](https://www.vertiv.com/en-us/insights/articles/educational-articles/proving-the-ai-factory-before-it-is-built/ "Vertiv, Proving the AI factory before it is built, 18 November 2026."), walked a recorded digital twin, a live load-bank test, and operating modes in simulation. Its [application brief](https://www.vertiv.com/4a6817/globalassets/content---assets-2025/documents/vertiv-ai-factory-readiness_application-brief_gl-english_rev0926_sl-80657.pdf "Vertiv application brief, revision 09/26.") puts 2026 testing at row-level bulk load, with full dynamic AI-server emulation due in early 2027. Claims of much shorter validation and of up to 30% more first-year token output are modeled, not ATLAS measurements. This is a closed, lab-scale capability, not a campus twin or a closed operational control loop.

Implication: A design-time twin plus a pod-scale load test can move integration risk off the construction site. It does not yet replace a telemetry-calibrated twin that predicts or controls a live hall under inference transients.

**Sources:** [16 Nov release](https://www.vertiv.com/en-us/about/news-and-events/corporate-news/2026/vertiv-atlas-proves-ai-infrastructure-ahead-of-silicon/ "Primary announcement, DSX stack, and no-GPU emulator statement."); [18 Nov article](https://www.vertiv.com/en-us/insights/articles/educational-articles/proving-the-ai-factory-before-it-is-built/ "Walk-through sequence and modeled token-output figures."); [application brief](https://www.vertiv.com/4a6817/globalassets/content---assets-2025/documents/vertiv-ai-factory-readiness_application-brief_gl-english_rev0926_sl-80657.pdf "2026 bulk-load versus early-2027 dynamic-emulation timeline.").

### 4. ExaDigiT’s cooling surrogates were presented at SC26; the public paper is still pre-surrogate · 20 Nov 2026 · academia / digital twin

Nigatu, Brewer, McConnell, Anantharaj and Fiore ([University of Trento](https://www.unitn.it/ "University of Trento.") and [Oak Ridge National Laboratory](https://www.ornl.gov/ "Oak Ridge National Laboratory.")) presented “Fast and Accurate ML Surrogates from Simulation: An HPC Liquid-Cooling Models Case Study” at SC26 on 20 November ([OLCF listing](https://www.olcf.ornl.gov/sc26/ "Oak Ridge Leadership Computing Facility page listing the 20 November, 9:20 a.m. CST workshop talk.")). Trained-surrogate metrics from the talk were not posted in a verifiable full paper. The checkable companion is their [GreenSys ’26](https://doi.org/10.1145/3802973.3804456 "Nigatu et al., Foundations for FMU Surrogate Model Design in ExaDigiT, GreenSys ’26, 27–30 April 2026, DOI 10.1145/3802973.3804456.") analysis of [FMU](https://fmi-standard.org/ "Functional Mock-up Unit (FMU): a packaged simulation model under the Functional Mock-up Interface standard.") cooling data from three IBM POWER9 systems in ExaDigiT — Marconi100 (49 [CDUs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_cooling#Data_centers "Coolant distribution unit (CDU): a heat-exchange skid that couples a facility cooling loop to IT liquid cooling.")), Summit (257) and Lassen (44). It finds a decoupling between a load-following primary loop and a nearly constant secondary loop, level-driven dynamics (median rate/level ratio 0.33; 54% of pathways show higher-order transients), and negligible inter-CDU coupling (asymmetry ratio 0.95). The [FMU dataset](https://zenodo.org/doi/10.5281/zenodo.19595930 "Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.19595930, made available 1 April 2026.") is public and can couple to open-source [RAPS](https://github.com/ExaDigiT/RAPS "ExaDigiT Resource Allocator and Power Simulator: schedules or replays workloads, estimates dynamic power, and can step an FMU cooling model."). This is simulation-derived analysis, not a telemetry-calibrated live-plant twin, and it does not close a control loop.

Implication: The open stack is being prepared for faster, physics-regularized cooling surrogates. Until the SC26 trained-surrogate numbers are posted, the verified public result is design guidance and data, not a validated real-time twin.

**Sources:** [OLCF SC26 listing](https://www.olcf.ornl.gov/sc26/ "Talk title, authors, and 20 November time."); [GreenSys ’26 PDF](https://iris.unitn.it/retrieve/d8d6c88c-f7cb-4d6b-bc21-a7e2ccc5b7ac/3802973.3804456.pdf "Source of the CDU counts and the four design findings."); [Zenodo dataset](https://zenodo.org/doi/10.5281/zenodo.19595930 "Public FMU dataset.").

## Bottom line

On-board robot computers now have measured joules, not only latency: a compiled VLA path can draw more watts and still spend fewer joules per call, and a detector that runs less often can cut power without cutting mission energy. Vertiv is proving power and cooling together in a lab that still uses 2026 bulk loads. ExaDigiT has an open cooling-twin stack with public data, but not yet a verified real-time, telemetry-closed loop.


## Glossary

- [**CDU**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_cooling#Data_centers): Coolant distribution unit.
- [**ExaDigiT**](https://exadigit.github.io/): Open-source digital-twin framework for liquid-cooled supercomputers.
