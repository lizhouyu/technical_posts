## Key developments

### 1. Vertiv fills 800 VDC at three scales · Nov 17 2026 · industry / rack–pod–hall

Vertiv's Nov 17 release, dated Columbus and showcased at SC26, takes the May PowerDirect 5000 sidecar and extends it to a compute pod and a data hall. Every rating below is a vendor claim. Pilots, technical validation and customer development are underway. Vertiv names no buyer, no site and no in-service date.

At the rack, the PowerDirect 5000 sits beside the IT rack. Input is 480 VAC. Output is 800 VDC or ±400 VDC, up to 800 kW, from 30 hot-pluggable 30 kW [power supply units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_supply_unit_%28computer%29 "Power supply unit (PSU): the converter module that produces the DC voltage the IT equipment uses."), with an integrated BBU designed for at least 50 seconds of hold-up. The PowerDirect 3000 is the shelf inside the compute rack: a 1U, 90 kW [DC-DC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC-to-DC_converter "DC-DC converter: a converter that changes one DC voltage to another. Here, 800 VDC down to 54 VDC.") converter, 800 VDC to 54 VDC, up to 98.5 percent peak efficiency, and 180 percent pulse power for 0.5 milliseconds. That pulse figure is a shelf claim. It is not a measured ride-through of a grid fault.

At the row, the PowerDirect 9000 is a 1.5 MW, 480 VAC-to-800 VDC center for a row or a pod. It feeds PowerDirect 3000 shelves over the Vertiv PowerBar Track DC [busway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busbar "Busway: a prefabricated conductor that carries high current along a row, here on DC, in place of a cable bundle per rack."), and it scales in 60 kW steps through 27 supplies. EnergyCore Li5 puts storage on that DC bus: 200 kW continuous, up to 98 percent conversion efficiency, and up to six cabinets in parallel with active current sharing.

At the hall, the PowerNexus MV SST is a 5 MW unit that converts medium-voltage AC to 800 VDC in one stage, in an outdoor liquid-cooled container, at up to 98 percent AC-to-DC efficiency. Vertiv sizes one unit for a 4.8 MW super-pod reference without chaining units. The company says the converter is bidirectional and can support grid power quality. Fault ride-through and "AI power smoothing" are stated only when external energy storage is coupled on the 800 VDC bus through a DC-DC converter. The smoothing claim is not a property of the SST alone.

The three levels are three procurement paths. The brownfield path, PowerDirect 5000 plus 3000, keeps the existing upstream AC and, Vertiv says, frees 8 to 16 rack units. The 9000 consolidates conversion ahead of the row. The SST is for purpose-built AI factories.

This SST is a different SKU from the PowerNexus MV UPS in the [Nov 13 brief](https://lizhouyu.github.io/technical_posts/posts/mv-ups-smoothing-aemc-ride-through-fair-energy-bills/ "Nov 13: PowerNexus MV UPS, which conditions a medium-voltage AC path and includes battery-backed operation."). The UPS conditions a medium-voltage AC path. Today's SST delivers an 800 VDC bus. Treating the November UPS smoothing claim as if it transferred to this transformer would over-read both releases.

### 4. Low-voltage ride-through as a DC-link power balance · submitted 14 Nov 2026 · academia / ride-through

Wang, Chen, Lin and Enjeti (Texas A&M), with Yazdanpanah, Cheng and Abhyankar (ERCOT), frame LVRT as a power balance on the DC link. Once the rectifier is at its current limit, power drawn from the grid falls in proportion to grid voltage. The DC-link capacitor covers the deficit only briefly. If nothing else supplies it, DC-link voltage falls and an undervoltage trip disconnects the facility. The IT load can keep running on a battery after that trip, which is how a hall disappears from the grid while its GPUs stay up. Every number in this item is from the authors' electromagnetic-transient study.

The paper cites two disturbances as context. [NERC](https://www.nerc.com/ "North American Electric Reliability Corporation: the international reliability organization for the bulk power system in the United States, Canada and northern Baja California.") documented about 1,500 MW of simultaneous data-center load reduction after a normally cleared fault in July 2024. ERCOT identified 26 data-center or crypto-mining trips during normal voltage disturbances since 2023. [ERCOT NOGRR 282](https://www.ercot.com/ "ERCOT Nodal Operating Guide Revision Request 282, as cited by the authors."), as cited by the authors, calls for the load to stay connected for prescribed durations. Power may fall with voltage between 0.5 and 0.9 per unit. After voltage recovers above 0.9 per unit, power must be restored to 90 percent of the pre-disturbance level within 2 seconds.

The study uses the TAMU-ERCOT [PSCAD](https://www.pscad.com/ "PSCAD: electromagnetic-transient simulation software used for interconnection studies.") AI data-center model: a 1 MW facility, a 1,000 V / 20 mF DC link, an 800 V trip, a 1.2 per-unit current limit, a 1 MW battery, a 0.4 per-unit IT floor, and the ERCOT Large Load Model Quality Test. With no LVRT control, the DC link collapses and the rectifier trips under a mild sag. IT continues on the battery. Grid power goes to zero. Battery support alone holds the DC link within ±5 percent of nominal, with no IT curtailment, and keeps grid power above the requirement through the whole test, including a near-zero-voltage fault. Curtailment alone, with the current setpoint at 1.1 per unit, rides through sags above about 0.36 per unit and trips on the zero-voltage fault because of the IT floor. Coordinated control splits the deficit with quadratic costs on battery power and on curtailment. In the authors' weights, shallow sags use curtailment alone, deeper sags share the increment, and the battery covers what remains once the floor is reached. That case also stays connected through the full test.

This is an EMT study of one modeled 1 MW hall, not a field trial, and the cost weights are assumed. With no added control the modeled hall trips on a mild sag, IT continues on the battery, and grid power goes to zero. Staying connected, in the other cases, means covering the deficit from the battery or from IT curtailment. Vertiv's hall-scale SST states the same split from the product side. Smoothing and fault ride-through are claimed with external storage on the 800 VDC bus, through a DC-DC converter (see the Spotlight).

**Sources:** [arXiv 2611.19667](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19667 "Submitted 14 Nov 2026.") · [Texas A&M electrical engineering](https://engineering.tamu.edu/electrical/index.html) · [ERCOT](https://www.ercot.com/)

### 5. Flexible interconnection prices lost work, not lost megawatt-hours · submitted 14 Nov 2026 · academia / capping and flexibility

Mishra, Omidi and Almassalkhi (University of Vermont) define flexible interconnection as early access in exchange for a grid-import limit. The cost they count is work lost, not megawatt-hours. The paper cites H100 rental of about USD 3–5 per GPU-hour, which it normalizes to roughly USD 4,300–7,100 per MWh at 700 W, against about USD 85 per MWh of US industrial power. Jobs respond unevenly to the same GPU power cap. A real-time allocation that equalizes marginal value therefore spares jobs near their [service-level agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service-level_agreement "Service-level agreement (SLA): a committed service target. Here, a deadline the allocator tries not to miss.") deadline, and the authors also give planners a shedding-capability curve. The dollar figures are the paper's citations and normalization. The case-study percentages below are simulation.

On a one-year, 100 MW case, the proposed allocation lowers the annual cost of meeting grid limits by 12 percent versus priority tiers. Adding on-site generation and storage lowers that cost by a further 90 percent. On one summer weekday, 26.0 MWh curtailed rebounds as 28.7 MWh later when the site has no [behind-the-meter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_generation "Behind-the-meter: generation or storage on the customer's side of the utility meter.") supply: under a cap, the jobs are not energy-proportional, so the energy that returns exceeds the energy that was cut. With behind-the-meter supply, the rebound falls to 5.2 MWh.

Job values and profiles are assumed or model-generated. Firm load is treated as known. The allocator does not plan for announced events. The paper treats interactive inference as inflexible. Item 6 relaxes that assumption by giving flexible inference requests a deferral window inside the dispatch. Read against items 2 and 3, the practical point is that Firm Service Step Up, GRITS, and Non-Firm Withdrawal Service all need a curtailment the operator can trust. This paper's answer is to spend the cap on the jobs with the lowest marginal value, and to keep a battery or on-site generation for the hours when lost work would otherwise rebound.

**Sources:** [arXiv 2611.19073](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19073 "Submitted 14 Nov 2026.") · [University of Vermont](https://www.uvm.edu/cems/ebe)

### 6. Inference routing inside an optimal power flow on a 7,830-bus PJM model · submitted 14 Nov 2026 · academia / inference and grid

Ma and Qiu (Nanyang Technological University) and Liu (Cornell) put inference routing inside an optimal power flow. Requests carry [time-to-first-token](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_to_first_token "Time to first token (TTFT): latency from request arrival until the first generated token.") and [inter-token latency](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19125 "Inter-token latency (ITL): the time between successive generated tokens.") limits, and flexible requests have a deferral window. The network is a 7,830-bus PJM model, with 333 data centers mapped to 138 buses. Each hall reserves 10 percent of its capacity for inference, served as Llama-3.1-405B replicas on eight H100s. The simulated week is 10–16 July 2023. In that week, median ITL is 36.3 ms for interactive requests and 48.0 ms for flexible requests, and both classes are at 80 ms TTFT. These are simulation results on 2023 profiles, not a live dispatch.

Two benchmarks bound the method. Computing-based routing sends a request to the nearest reachable hall with spare GPUs. Ex-ante [locational marginal price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locational_marginal_pricing "Locational marginal price (LMP): the wholesale energy price at a bus, in this paper computed without the inference load and then used to route requests.") routing sends it to the cheapest reachable hall, using prices computed without the inference load. At a 4 percent inference energy share, the weekly system-cost increase versus the proposed OPF with a 12-hour deferral window is 7.1 percent for computing-based routing, 6.6 percent for ex-ante LMP routing, and 5.7 percent for the proposed method. At 6 percent and 8 percent, infeasible hours are 20 and 83 for computing-based routing, 10 and 16 for ex-ante LMP routing, and 0 for the proposed OPF. The proposed OPF sends some Northern Virginia demand to still-reachable halls in Ohio and western Pennsylvania. In the tested range of tokens per replica and activation power, relaxing ITL reduces cost more than relaxing TTFT. Longer deferral windows save more money and can raise night-time hall peaks.

The study is a convex simulation, not an operational trial. It relaxes item 5's treatment of interactive inference as inflexible: flexible requests have a deferral window, and the proposed OPF can move some of that work to still-reachable halls. In this setup each hall reserves 10 percent of capacity for inference.

## Spotlight: ride-through as a DC-link balance

Once the grid-side rectifier is against its current limit, grid power falls in proportion to grid voltage:

$$
P_{\mathrm{grid}} = I_{\mathrm{grid}}^{\max} \cdot V_{\mathrm{grid}}
$$

IT still wants $$P_{\mathrm{IT}}$$. The deficit $$P_{\mathrm{IT}} - P_{\mathrm{grid}}$$ has to come from the DC-link capacitor, from the UPS battery, or from IT curtailment. If it does not, $$V_{\mathrm{DC}}$$ falls and the undervoltage trip takes the facility off the grid. The capacitor term is brief. That is why the no-control case in [arXiv 2611.19667](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19667 "The EMT study behind this balance: a modeled 1 MW hall on the TAMU-ERCOT PSCAD model.") collapses the DC link under a mild sag, leaves IT on the battery, and drops grid power to zero.

The same identity sorts the other simulated cases. Battery support alone covers the deficit with no IT curtailment: the DC link stays within ±5 percent of nominal, and grid power stays above the requirement through the test, including a near-zero-voltage fault. Curtailment alone, with the current setpoint at 1.1 per unit, rides through sags above about 0.36 per unit and trips on the zero-voltage fault because of the 0.4 per-unit IT floor. Coordinated control, under the authors' quadratic weights, uses curtailment on shallow sags and shares deeper sags, with the battery covering whatever remains at the floor. Those weights are assumed, and the plant is one modeled 1 MW hall.

Vertiv's Nov 17 SST states the hardware version of the same split. Fault ride-through and "AI power smoothing" are claimed only when external energy storage is coupled on the 800 VDC bus through a DC-DC converter. External storage on the bus is what the release attaches to that claim. The SST stage alone is not stated to supply $$P_{\mathrm{IT}} - P_{\mathrm{grid}}$$ during a sag. The PowerDirect 5000's integrated BBU, designed for at least 50 seconds of hold-up, is storage at the sidecar, on a different SKU from the hall-scale SST, and on a different product from the Nov 13 medium-voltage UPS. ERCOT NOGRR 282, as the authors cite it, allows power to fall with voltage between 0.5 and 0.9 per unit and then asks for 90 percent of pre-disturbance power within 2 seconds after recovery above 0.9 per unit. Meeting that restoration is a battery or a curtailment plan, not a property of the AC-to-DC stage by itself.

## Key developments (continued)

### 7. A ceasing-and-reconnection model that can be checked against synchrophasors · submitted 16 Nov 2026 · adjacent / grid modeling

Odunlami and Netto ([arXiv 2611.21144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21144 "Submitted 16 Nov 2026. PERC1: a hybrid-automaton model of power-electronic loads that cease and reconnect.")) write PERC1, for Power Electronic Reconnecting and Ceasing, as a model of large converter loads, including data centers. It is a [hybrid automaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_system "Hybrid automaton: a model that combines continuous dynamics with discrete modes, here voltage thresholds and timers that switch a load between connected, ceased and ramping."): voltage thresholds and timers, and a ramp when the load reconnects. Parameters are identified from [synchrophasor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phasor_measurement_unit "Synchrophasor: a time-synchronized phasor measured by a phasor measurement unit (PMU).") records.

The structural results are about what one disturbance can identify. Detection delay and actuation delay are identifiable only as a sum, unless a second dip interrupts the reconnection ramp. A single disturbance leaves one unidentifiable direction in each threshold-delay pair. Two disturbances with different voltage slopes resolve that direction. A case study on synthetic 60-frame-per-second records recovers the ceasing and reconnection dynamics and flags parameters that are poorly observable.

This is a methods paper on synthetic measurements, not a fit to a named hall. It matters for the ride-through packages in items 2 through 4. A ceasing-and-reconnection model that an interconnection study will check against [PMU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phasor_measurement_unit "Phasor measurement unit (PMU): a device that records time-synchronized voltage and current phasors.") records has to be identifiable from the records those meters actually capture. One dip, on this result, is not enough to separate a threshold from its delay.

**Sources:** [arXiv 2611.21144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21144 "Submitted 16 Nov 2026.")

### 8. Switching the carbon-intensity source changes the winning site in 43.50 percent of cases · submitted 16 Nov 2026 · adjacent / carbon-aware computing

Ferreira, Blanco, Castrillo, López García, Wu and Ilager ([arXiv 2611.20830](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20830 "Submitted 16 Nov 2026. Carbon-intensity source choice changes a reproduced carbon-aware placement.")) harmonize Wattnet and Electricity Maps [carbon-intensity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emission_intensity "Carbon intensity (CI): the emissions associated with a unit of electricity, here as published by two different data products.") traces across five Central European zones and reproduce CarbonEdge's placement. Switching the CI source changes the dominant placement site in 43.50 percent of cases. It also changes the reported operational outcomes, even where the rank correlation between the two traces is high. The implication they draw is to state which CI product was used, and to treat CI uncertainty as part of the decision rather than as a footnote.

The result is tied to one reproduced system and five zones. It does not say which product is more accurate. A carbon-aware placement, including one combined with the price-aware inference routing in item 6, should name the CI product and treat that uncertainty as part of the decision.

**Sources:** [arXiv 2611.20830](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20830 "Submitted 16 Nov 2026.")

## Bottom line

Vertiv now states an 800 VDC portfolio at three scales, from an 800 kW sidecar with at least 50 seconds of hold-up to a 5 MW medium-voltage solid-state transformer. The transformer claim and the ride-through paper agree on the deficit: once grid current is limited, $$P_{\mathrm{grid}} = I_{\mathrm{grid}}^{\max} \cdot V_{\mathrm{grid}}$$, and $$P_{\mathrm{IT}} - P_{\mathrm{grid}}$$ comes from a battery, from IT curtailment, or the DC link trips. MISO and NYISO have filed the contractual form of that flexibility — step-up and interim service, a grid-responsive companion, and firm and non-firm withdrawal — as proposals, with Q1 2027 named only as MISO's implementation-planning horizon. Spending the cap on the right jobs, and routing flexible inference inside the power flow, is what makes those services a compute decision rather than only a converter rating. A ceasing model checked against one synchrophasor dip can leave a threshold and a delay tangled. A carbon-aware placement can change its winner when the intensity product changes. Industrial figures above are vendor claims or filing summaries. Academic figures are simulations.

## Glossary

- [**800 VDC**](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/technologies/800-vdc-architecture/ "800 V direct current distribution for dense AI racks."): High-voltage DC distribution aimed at lower current than low-voltage AC or 54 V paths.
- [**BBU**](https://www.opencompute.org/ "Battery backup unit."): Battery backup unit. On the PowerDirect 5000, Vertiv designs the integrated BBU for at least 50 seconds of hold-up.
- [**Behind-the-meter**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_generation): Generation or storage on the customer side of the meter.
- [**Busway**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busbar): Prefabricated conductor along a row. Vertiv's PowerBar Track is the DC busway in today's release.
- [**CAISO**](https://www.caiso.com/ "California Independent System Operator."): Grid operator for most of California. Its Nov 16 FILI proposal was covered in yesterday's brief.
- [**Carbon intensity**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emission_intensity): Emissions associated with a unit of electricity. Item 8 compares two products that publish it.
- [**DC-DC**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC-to-DC_converter): A converter between two DC voltages. The PowerDirect 3000 steps 800 VDC to 54 VDC. External storage couples to the SST bus through one.
- [**DC link**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectifier "The capacitor bus downstream of a rectifier."): The capacitor bus between the grid-side rectifier and the IT converters. Ride-through in item 4 is a power balance on this bus.
- [**EMT**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMTP): Electromagnetic-transient simulation, the class of study in arXiv 2611.19667.
- [**ERCOT**](https://www.ercot.com/): Electric Reliability Council of Texas.
- [**Fast Energization**](https://lizhouyu.github.io/technical_posts/posts/caiso-20mw-pjm-fast-energization-gfm-halls/ "Covered in the Nov 17 brief."): PJM's proposed one-year study path for generation and large loads. Covered yesterday.
- [**FERC**](https://www.ferc.gov/): US Federal Energy Regulatory Commission.
- [**FILI**](https://lizhouyu.github.io/technical_posts/posts/caiso-20mw-pjm-fast-energization-gfm-halls/ "Flexible Interim Load Interconnection, covered in the Nov 17 brief."): CAISO's proposed Flexible Interim Load Interconnection. Covered yesterday.
- [**Firm Service Step Up**](https://www.misoenergy.org/engage/MISO-Dashboard/firm-service-step-up-for-large-load-interconnections/): MISO's proposal for firm service at a reduced level before all upgrades finish.
- [**GRITS**](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/pjm-rto-large-load-interconnection-ferc-show-cause/874872/ "Grid Responsive Integration Transmission Service, as reported by Utility Dive."): Grid Responsive Integration Transmission Service. Utility Dive describes it as MISO's companion filing, under which large loads could limit use.
- [**H100**](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/h100/): NVIDIA Hopper data-center GPU. Items 5 and 6 use it as the rental and replica GPU.
- [**Hybrid automaton**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_system): A model with continuous dynamics and discrete modes. PERC1 uses voltage thresholds, timers and a reconnection ramp.
- [**ITL**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19125 "Inter-token latency."): Inter-token latency, the time between successive generated tokens.
- [**LMP**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locational_marginal_pricing): Locational marginal price. Ex-ante LMP routing uses prices computed without the inference load.
- [**LVRT**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fault_ride-through): Low-voltage ride-through.
- [**MISO**](https://www.misoenergy.org/): Midcontinent Independent System Operator.
- [**MV**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltage#Medium_voltage): Medium voltage, the AC level a large hall takes from the utility and the input of the PowerNexus MV SST.
- [**NERC**](https://www.nerc.com/): North American Electric Reliability Corporation. The ride-through paper cites NERC's account of the July 2024 data-center load reduction.
- [**NITS**](https://www.ferc.gov/ "Network Integration Transmission Service."): Network Integration Transmission Service. MISO proposes Interim Non-Firm NITS during build-out.
- [**NOGRR 282**](https://www.ercot.com/ "ERCOT Nodal Operating Guide Revision Request 282, as cited by the authors."): ERCOT operating-guide revision on ride-through, as cited by the authors of arXiv 2611.19667.
- [**NYISO**](https://www.nyiso.com/): New York Independent System Operator.
- [**OATT**](https://www.ferc.gov/ "Open-Access Transmission Tariff."): Open-Access Transmission Tariff. NYISO proposes revisions to it.
- [**OPF**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimal_power_flow): Optimal power flow. Item 6 routes inference inside one.
- [**PJM**](https://www.pjm.com/): Regional grid operator for 13 eastern US states and D.C. Item 6 uses a 7,830-bus model of its network.
- [**PMU**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phasor_measurement_unit): Phasor measurement unit, the recorder of synchrophasors.
- [**PSCAD**](https://www.pscad.com/): Electromagnetic-transient simulation tool used for the TAMU-ERCOT data-center model.
- [**PSU**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_supply_unit_%28computer%29): Power supply unit. The PowerDirect 5000 uses 30 hot-pluggable 30 kW units.
- [**pu**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per-unit_system): Per-unit, a fraction of a nominated base such as nominal voltage or rated power.
- [**Rectifier**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectifier): Grid-side converter that feeds the DC link. At its current limit, grid power falls with grid voltage.
- [**Section 205**](https://www.ferc.gov/ "Federal Power Act section 205."): The Federal Power Act provision for filing proposed rates. New York transmission owners made concurrent Section 205 filings on reserved matters.
- [**SLA**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service-level_agreement): Service-level agreement. Item 5's allocator spares jobs near an SLA deadline.
- [**SPP**](https://www.spp.org/): Southwest Power Pool. Nov 17 reports still had its large-load filing due later that week.
- [**SST**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid-state_transformer): Solid-state transformer. Today's PowerNexus MV SST converts medium-voltage AC to 800 VDC in one stage. It is not the Nov 13 PowerNexus MV UPS.
- [**TTFT**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_to_first_token): Time to first token.
- [**UPS**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uninterruptible_power_supply): Uninterruptible power supply. The Nov 13 PowerNexus MV UPS conditions a medium-voltage AC path.
