## At a glance

- **Advocates want data-center load out of PJM’s December auction.** An 18 November letter asks the [PJM](https://www.pjm.com/ "PJM Interconnection: regional grid operator for 13 eastern US states and DC") Board to seek [FERC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Energy_Regulatory_Commission "Federal Energy Regulatory Commission: the US regulator of interstate wholesale electricity markets and transmission.") authorization to postpone the 9–15 December 2026 [Base Residual Auction](https://www.pjm.com/markets-and-operations/rpm/ "Base Residual Auction (BRA): PJM’s three-year-forward capacity auction under its Reliability Pricing Model.") for 2029/30. The [IMM](https://www.monitoringanalytics.com/ "Monitoring Analytics: PJM’s Independent Market Monitor.") cites USD 29.4 billion already and another USD 5 to USD 10 billion on the current forecast; PJM has not agreed to delay.

- **North Carolina floors the data-center bill at 75% of contract demand.** On 16 November the [NCUC](https://www.ncuc.gov/ "North Carolina Utilities Commission (NCUC): the state regulator that issued the 16 November 2026 Duke rate orders.") approved a [Duke](https://investors.duke-energy.com/news/news-details/2026/North-Carolina-regulators-approve-more-cost-effective-path-to-strengthen-reliability-and-power-the-states-future/default.aspx "Duke Energy’s 17 November 2026 IR release announcing the NCUC orders.") DC Schedule with a 15-year term above 100 MW, a 75% minimum billing demand, interim curtailment, and [ride-through](https://lizhouyu.github.io/technical_posts/posts/caiso-20mw-pjm-fast-energization-gfm-halls/ "Ride-through: remaining connected through a grid fault or voltage sag.").

- **New papers number an hourly energy budget and voltage at a GW-scale POI.** [eFAIR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24119 "Peng, Jiang, Wang and Yuan, eFAIR, arXiv 2611.24119, announced 19 Nov 2026.") holds hourly commitments within 0.26%. Ahmed and Manshadi (arXiv [2611.23771](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23771 "Ahmed and Manshadi, arXiv 2611.23771, announced 19 Nov 2026.")) find GW-scale [POI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_grid "Point of interconnection (POI): the bus where a large load formally connects to the transmission system.") flexibility still needs a [BESS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid_energy_storage "Battery energy storage system (BESS): a battery plus inverter used for backup, ramp smoothing or grid services.") inverter or [STATCOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Static_synchronous_compensator "Static synchronous compensator (STATCOM): a power-electronic device that injects or absorbs reactive power to support voltage.") to hold voltage off the 0.95 [pu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per-unit_system "Per-unit (pu) system: voltages as a fraction of a chosen base; 0.95 pu is 95% of nominal.") limit. [NYISO](https://www.nyiso.com/ "New York Independent System Operator.")’s 2026 RNA, missed yesterday, says peak curtailment of new planned data-center loads would cut the 2036 need from about 1,850 MW to 850 MW.

## Key developments

### 1. Advocates and the IMM ask PJM to postpone the 2029/30 auction until data-center load is removed · 18–19 Nov 2026 · industry / grid

Consumer-advocate offices in seven PJM states, plus the Illinois Citizens Utility Board, [wrote the PJM Board on 18 November](https://opc.maryland.gov/Portals/0/Files/Publications/Others/FIN%20PJM%20Board%20Letter%20re%20December%202026%20RPM%20auction%20(002).pdf "18 November 2026 letter to the PJM Board, hosted by the Maryland Office of People’s Counsel.") to seek FERC authorization to postpone the 9–15 December BRA for 2029/30 “until data center loads, both real and speculative, can be removed from the load forecast,” and to run a separate [Reliability Backstop Auction](https://www.monitoringanalytics.com/reports/PJM_State_of_the_Market/2026/2026q3-som-pjm.pdf "The IMM’s recommended separate auction for forecast data-center load without a bilateral capacity contract.") for data centers without bilateral contracts. The cost figures are the IMM’s, not a FERC finding: USD 29.4 billion already, and another USD 5 to USD 10 billion if the auction uses PJM’s 2026 load forecast. They ask for a written Board response by 23 November. PJM spokesman Jeffrey Shields told [*Utility Dive*](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/ratepayer-advocates-pjm-market-monitor-capacity-auction/875165/ "Howland, Utility Dive, 19 Nov 2026: quotes PJM spokesman Jeffrey Shields.") on 19 November that postponing the auction “this close to its execution could destabilize the market and increase the risk of power interruptions.” The Board has not postponed the auction.

**Sources:** [18 Nov letter](https://opc.maryland.gov/Portals/0/Files/Publications/Others/FIN%20PJM%20Board%20Letter%20re%20December%202026%20RPM%20auction%20(002).pdf "Primary request."); [IMM Q3 2026 State of the Market, 12 Nov](https://www.monitoringanalytics.com/reports/PJM_State_of_the_Market/2026/2026q3-som-pjm.pdf "Source of the USD 29.4 billion and USD 5 to USD 10 billion figures."); [*Utility Dive*, 19 Nov](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/ratepayer-advocates-pjm-market-monitor-capacity-auction/875165/ "PJM’s on-the-record response.").

### 2. NCUC approves Duke’s DC Schedule: a 75% minimum bill, curtailment, and ride-through · 16–17 Nov 2026 · industry / retail-grid

The 16 November orders, announced in Duke’s 17 November [IR release](https://investors.duke-energy.com/news/news-details/2026/North-Carolina-regulators-approve-more-cost-effective-path-to-strengthen-reliability-and-power-the-states-future/default.aspx "Duke Energy IR release, 17 November 2026.") and [Form 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/17797/000110465926128747/tm2629986d1_8k.htm "Duke Energy Form 8-K, event date 16 November 2026, filed 17 November."), create a DC Schedule for certain data-center customers. Duke “is still assessing that portion of the Orders,” with rates expected 1 January 2027. The [DEC fact sheet](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/17797/000110465926128747/tm2629986d1_ex99-1.htm "Exhibit 99.1 to the 8-K: DC Schedule terms as stated by Duke.") lists a 15-year term above 100 MW, a 75% minimum billing demand, interim curtailment, [CIAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contribution_in_aid_of_construction "Contribution in aid of construction (CIAC): an upfront customer payment toward dedicated facilities."), early-exit damages, and a stranded-generation charge. The [DEC order](https://bloximages.newyork1.vip.townnews.com/journalnow.com/content/tncms/assets/v3/editorial/d/32/d32d7e98-15ce-45da-98a6-6db9de4f9397/6afca3ecab161.pdf.pdf "NCUC Order, Docket No. E-7, Sub 1329 and Sub 1355, 16 November 2026.") defines a DC customer as a load of at least 50 MW that functions as a data center, applies the XL Schedule’s ride-through provisions to new DC customers, records 6.7 GW of signed ESAs from March 2024 through April 2026 (92% data centers) and 7.9 GW in advanced development, and approves a proxy stranded-generation charge of USD 67,357 per MW-year, pending a later stakeholder process. A hall that caps or smooths below reserved demand will still pay for most of that reservation. Implementation is not final.

**Sources:** [Duke IR, 17 Nov](https://investors.duke-energy.com/news/news-details/2026/North-Carolina-regulators-approve-more-cost-effective-path-to-strengthen-reliability-and-power-the-states-future/default.aspx "First-party announcement."); [Exhibit 99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/17797/000110465926128747/tm2629986d1_ex99-1.htm "DC Schedule terms as stated by Duke."); [NCUC order, 16 Nov](https://bloximages.newyork1.vip.townnews.com/journalnow.com/content/tncms/assets/v3/editorial/d/32/d32d7e98-15ce-45da-98a6-6db9de4f9397/6afca3ecab161.pdf.pdf "Findings 79, 86, 88 and 96.").

### 3. eFAIR serves LLM requests under a hard hourly electricity commitment · 19 Nov 2026 · academia / inference capping

You Peng, Youhe Jiang (equal contribution), Chen Wang and Binhang Yuan at [HKUST](https://www.hkust.edu.hk/ "Hong Kong University of Science and Technology.") (arXiv [2611.24119](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24119 "Peng, Jiang, Wang and Yuan, eFAIR, arXiv 2611.24119, announced 19 Nov 2026.")) study LLM serving when a datacenter has “committed to energy purchases for individual hours” in day-ahead markets. eFAIR jointly sets dispatch, electricity-budget allocation, and GPU frequency under that constraint. On H20 and H200 configurations, each with 32 replicas, it “improves the tenth percentile of normalized throughput by 28.0%–31.0% over the best budget-feasible baseline, while keeping absolute deviations from the hourly electricity commitment below 0.26%.” A cluster study, not a hall measurement; the 28–31% figure is *normalized* throughput, not raw tokens per second.

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.24119](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24119 "Abstract and PDF for Peng, Jiang, Wang and Yuan.").

### 4. At a GW-scale POI, watts of flexibility are not enough without reactive support · 19 Nov 2026 · academia / ramp smoothing and grid

Eihab E. E. Ahmed ([San Diego State University](https://www.sdsu.edu/ "San Diego State University.") and UC San Diego) and Saeed Manshadi (SDSU) (arXiv [2611.23771](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23771 "Ahmed and Manshadi, arXiv 2611.23771, announced 19 Nov 2026.")) embed an [AIDC](https://lizhouyu.github.io/technical_posts/posts/aidc-power-stack-and-shortlist/ "AI data center (AIDC): a facility built to host large-scale AI training and inference.") model with inference and training pools, a BESS with reactive capability, and a STATCOM in a five-minute AC optimal power flow on the [RTS-GMLC](https://github.com/GridMod/RTS-GMLC "RTS-GMLC: an updated IEEE Reliability Test System, used as the paper’s transmission test case.") system. Active-power flexibility “mitigates local price spikes and relieves congestion,” but “reactive support from the BESS inverter or a STATCOM is needed to keep the POI voltage away from sagging around its 0.95 pu limit.” “Cost-driven charging of large batteries can induce new morning congestion,” and “storage largely substitutes for wider workload deferral.” Figure 1: 0.957 pu with flexibility alone, 0.982 pu with a BESS inverter at ±0.95 power factor, and 0.993 pu with a STATCOM. Simulation, not a measured hall; a battery sized for energy shifting is not automatically a voltage solution.

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.23771](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23771 "Abstract and PDF, including the 0.957 / 0.982 / 0.993 pu voltages.").

### 5. NYISO’s first statewide resource deficiency, and a 1,000 MW flexibility case · 17–18 Nov 2026 · adjacent / resource adequacy

NYISO’s 2026 [Reliability Needs Assessment](https://www.nyiso.com/documents/20142/2248793/2026RNA-Report-Final.pdf "NYISO 2026 Reliability Needs Assessment, cover date 17 November 2026."), issued with an 18 November [press release](https://www.nyiso.com/-/press-release-nyiso-report-identifies-first-ever-statewide-resource-deficiency "NYISO press release, 18 November 2026."), finds a statewide resource-adequacy deficiency beginning in 2033: “initially 225 MW and rapidly gets worse through 2036 with a deficiency greater than 1,850 MW.” Demand growth is “especially driven by approximately 2,700 MW of planned large loads, including … 1,700 MW of computational data centers.” “If all new planned data center large loads (excluding those currently in service) are flexible and curtailed during peak demand periods, the statewide resource need in 2036 would reduce from approximately 1,850 MW to 850 MW.” The fact sheet states the 2033 shortfall as 200 MW; this brief uses the report’s 225 MW. A planning study and a bounding assumption, not a 2026 shortage or a signed tariff, and distinct from NYISO’s already-covered 16 November withdrawal-service filing.

**Sources:** [NYISO press release, 18 Nov](https://www.nyiso.com/-/press-release-nyiso-report-identifies-first-ever-statewide-resource-deficiency "First-party announcement."); [2026 RNA report](https://www.nyiso.com/documents/20142/2248793/2026RNA-Report-Final.pdf "Source of the 225 MW, 1,850 MW, 1,700 MW and 1,850-to-850 MW figures.").

## Bottom line

Policy documents try to keep speculative or uncapped AI load from being socialized: out of PJM’s December auction, or under a 75% minimum bill and ride-through in North Carolina. An inference cluster can track an hourly energy purchase to within a quarter of a percent, but a GW-scale hall that only shifts watts can still sag the POI voltage. NYISO’s 1,850-to-850 MW case is the planning version of that bargain. IMM and Duke figures are estimates or still under implementation; academic numbers are simulations.
